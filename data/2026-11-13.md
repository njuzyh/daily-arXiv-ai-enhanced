Abstract: Inter-cluster forwarding in wireless sensor networks can concentrate energy consumption on frequently selected relays, while hard energy thresholds restrict forwarding choices as residual energy declines. We propose Hierarchical Energy-balanced Routing Optimization based on a dueling double deep Q-network (D3QN), termed HERO-D3QN. Given the cluster structure, the method combines direction- and cost-based relay screening with energy-aware soft constraints. Relays below an adaptive energy threshold remain eligible if the other selection criteria are met. An enhanced state representation captures relay energy, progress toward the sink, potential forwarding load, and hotspot risk, and a multi-objective reward guides learning. In the comparison with D3QN using the same node locations, HERO-D3QN delayed first-node death (FND) from time step 629 to 1269 and reduced peak residual energy variance by 55.5%. In the ablation study, the full method achieved the latest FND and half-node death (HND) and the largest cumulative transmitted volume (170.52 Mbit) among the tested variants. Across five seed settings in which node locations and the random processes used in training and evaluation varied jointly, the coefficients of variation of FND, HND, and transmitted volume were 8.26%, 2.75%, and 3.88%, respectively. These results demonstrate the effectiveness of HERO-D3QN in delaying early node failure and reducing peak energy dispersion.

Abstract: Integrated Access and Backhaul (IAB) is a key enabler for cost-effective millimeter-wave (mmWave) densification, yet practical deployments are strongly constrained by the spatial availability of fiber-connected IAB donors. Variations in donor topologies reshape both the initial coverage footprint and the feasible multi-hop backhaul expansion patterns, causing planning policies trained on a single topology to generalize poorly. This paper studies cross-topology generalization for resilience-aware IAB deployment under coverage and transport feasibility requirements. We formulate IAB deployment as a constrained Markov decision process that couples access coverage satisfaction with multi-hop transport feasibility, including hop-by-hop rate-budget degradation and a minimum parent-connectivity requirement for fault tolerance. To solve the resulting large discrete planning problem, we propose a graph-based actor-critic approach that uses a Graph Attention Network v2 (GATv2) encoder with Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO), and we develop a multi-stage curriculum learning strategy that progressively increases constraint difficulty and scenario diversity for stable training and transferable policies. In a 60 GHz urban simulation over a 1000 m x 1000 m area with multiple donor layouts, the trained policy satisfies the minimum coverage requirement of 98% with fewer deployed IAB nodes than learning and heuristic baselines, and maintains higher coverage under progressive backhaul failures. The results demonstrate that curriculum-trained graph-based reinforcement learning can improve deployment efficiency and resilience while transferring across heterogeneous IAB donor topologies without retraining.

Abstract: Mobile edge computing (MEC)-empowered spaceair-ground integrated Internet-of-Things (IoT) networks (SAGIINs) provide a promising solution for computation-intensive tasks generated by mobile IoT devices (IDs). However, task offloading in SAGIINs is challenging due to time-varying IDto-unmanned aerial vehicle (UAV) channels, intermittent LEO satellite service availability, and the coupling among UAV trajectory, communication, computation, and energy decisions. This paper investigates an energy-efficient task offloading and UAV trajectory planning problem in an MEC-empowered SAGIIN, where one UAV acts as both an aerial relay and an MEC server, and multiple LEO satellites provide supplementary computing resources. The UAV trajectory planning, task splitting, and computing resource allocation are jointly optimized to minimize the total system energy consumption under task completion, communication, computation, flight region, and satellite service window constraints. The resulting non-convex problem is decomposed into a UAV trajectory planning subproblem and a task splitting and computing resource allocation subproblem. To solve the former, a retrieval-augmented generation (RAG)-assisted large language model (LLM)-enhanced deep deterministic policy gradient (DDPG) algorithm, termed RLD, is proposed. The latter is shown to be convex and can be solved by using CVXPY for a given UAV trajectory. A weighted retrieval score is further designed to balance ID mobility-pattern similarity and historical UAV trajectory energy performance. Simulation results show that RLD completes all computational tasks in previously unseen test scenarios while consuming less total energy than the benchmark algorithms.

Abstract: Emerging artificial intelligence workloads, particularly Large Language Models (LLMs), place unprecedented demands on GPU memory systems. Consequently, architectural innovations increasingly target the memory system to alleviate these bottlenecks. However, existing works still rely on outdated memory hierarchy models that fail to capture the complexities of modern hardware. This fidelity gap leads to inaccurate characterization and could misguide optimization strategies. To bridge this gap, we present GemAtlas, the first systematic reverse engineering of the memory hierarchy in modern NVIDIA GPUs. By developing fine-grained SASS-level benchmarking, we expose critical, previously undocumented micro-architectural details across different cache levels, including cache set associativity, slice-routing and set-indexing hash functions, replacement policies, coherence behavior, and interconnect topologies. We integrate these discovered micro-architecture details into the state-of-the-art GPU simulation framework. The final results demonstrate that GemAtlas reduces the mean absolute percentage error (MAPE) from 31.69% to 22.24% on average across four GPUs. Specifically, GemAtlas reduces the MAPE from 34.04% to 23.98% for real-world LLM operators.
