Abstract: Shared-medium Ethernet is gaining renewed attention across industrial domains, driven by the adoption of 10BASE-T1S. In such multidrop networks, collision-free and deterministic medium access is achieved through Physical Layer Collision Avoidance (PLCA). However, PLCA follows a node-based cyclic access structure that does not account for traffic priority, leading to high access latency for time-sensitive traffic and limited scalability with increasing network size. Existing PLCA extensions improve latency for selected devices but remain restricted to node-based access and do not support network-wide traffic differentiation. To address these limitations, this paper introduces a priority-based network access and scheduling mechanism for multidrop networks. The proposed approach decouples medium access from node-based ordering and enables class-based prioritization, allowing high-priority traffic from any device to be transmitted ahead of lower-priority traffic. Performance evaluation demonstrates that the proposed mechanism reduces the maximum observed access latency for high-priority traffic by over 65% compared to PLCA, with latency increasing by less than 1% as the network grows from 8 to 40 devices.
